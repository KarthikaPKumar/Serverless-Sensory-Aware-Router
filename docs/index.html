How are routes and walking instructions computed?

Dijkstra's algorithm runs locally over a central-Melbourne OpenStreetMap extract. Known foot restrictions, private access and unsuitable road classes are filtered out. Disconnected paths are excluded; we do not join gaps with invented connections. Some stairs are included, so this is not a wheelchair-accessible routing profile. Street segments are listed in travel order; this is not turn-by-turn navigation.

Addresses snap to the nearest graph node within 80 metres. The distance to that node is shown separately and is not a verified entrance connection. Walking time assumes 4.8 km/h and excludes waits and entrance connections.

How do observations influence the alternative?

Only readings observed within 30 minutes, from a snapshot fetched within 90 minutes, with at least 45 reporting minutes in the 60-minute window are used. This conservative coverage check avoids interpreting incomplete totals as low activity. An edge can use the nearest counter within 50 metres of its midpoint. This is proximity context, not proof that a reading represents that path or side of the road.

cost = segment metres × (1 + preference × activity)
activity = recorded passages / (recorded passages + 200)
unknown activity = 1 (maximum penalty)

The 200-passage scale is an explicit heuristic, not a trained model or calibrated safety threshold. Limited sensor coverage can dominate the alternative. We show coverage and never label an unmeasured route as quiet or safe. If no sufficiently recent and complete observations exist, the app offers only the shortest walking path.

How fresh are the pedestrian counts?

The refresh process requests the most recent 60-minute window. It removes exact duplicates, excludes conflicting duplicates and rejects invalid records. A sensor can omit minutes with no recorded passages, or miss data for other reasons. “Reporting minutes” is shown; missing records are not filled with zero.

Scheduled refreshes may be delayed. The app independently checks timestamps every minute, excludes stale observations from route costs, and shows missing data. A recently fetched file does not make an old observation fresh.

What about noise, lighting and live navigation?

No suitable live noise or illumination feed is connected. Those measurements are unknown and have no role in scoring. OpenStreetMap street tags are not decibel or lux readings. Routes are planning previews based on a map snapshot; follow current signs, crossings and access restrictions on the ground.
